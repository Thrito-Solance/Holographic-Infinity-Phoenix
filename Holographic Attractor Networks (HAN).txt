\begin{abstract}
We introduce a novel mathematical paradigm for high-performance computing that completely decouples computational integrity from microscopic transistor perfection. By formalising a distributed hardware network as a gauge-invariant scale-space fiber bundle, we show that information can be natively stored and processed as macro-scale geometric topologies rather than localized binary states. We deploy a non-commutative lattice adaptation of the Wetterich Exact Renormalization Group (ERG) equation over a Symmetric Normalized Graph Laplacian to continuously filter out high-frequency hardware noise caused by sub-optimal fabrication nodes. Finally, we formulate a phase-aligned degradation protocol via discrete Ricci metric deformations, proving that the network's memory capacity remains topologically protected under catastrophic, localized node failure rates up to a structural percolation threshold. This framework offers a rigorous theoretical workaround for data centers constrained by precision lithography limitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern deep learning hardware paradigms remain bound to the Von Neumann architecture and the strict requirement for microscopic physical perfection. As physical transistor scaling hits the quantum tunneling wall and global supply chains limit access to sub-3nm extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography, high-performance computing faces a structural crisis. 

This paper presents an alternative approach: a software-defined hardware architecture where memory and compute are fused into the macro-scale geometric topology of a network. By treating a distributed cluster of low-tier, noisy, or repurposed silicon nodes as a continuous differential manifold, we construct an environment where information is topologically protected against local hardware failure.

\section{The Scale-Space Fiber Bundle}
Rather than embedding data arrays into flat vector spaces ($\mathbb{R}^d$), we formalise the system state $\Psi$ as a smooth section of a principal fiber bundle $\mathcal{E}$ projected over a three-dimensional semantic base manifold $\mathcal{M}$.

\begin{equation}
\Psi \in \Gamma(\mathcal{E}), \quad \mathcal{E} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathcal{M}_{\text{semantic}}
\end{equation}

The coordinates on this bundle are defined by the four-vector $u = (x, y, z, s)$, where $(x, y, z) \in \mathcal{M}$ represent localized semantic coordinates and $s \in \mathbb{R}^+$ denotes a continuous scale-space parameter governing the structural resolution of the embedding.

To prevent phase drift and algebraic corruption as states translate across scale boundaries, we introduce an Ehresmann covariant connection $\omega_\mu$. The scale-space derivative is bounded by the covariant derivative:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_\mu \Psi = \left( \partial_\mu - i \omega_\mu \right) \Psi
\end{equation}

This ensures strict gauge invariance across all localized computing nodes.

\section{Bounded Discrete Graph Propagation}
To project this continuous manifold onto physical, hardware-constrained architectures (such as distributed server racks or programmable photonic integrated circuits), we define a graph network $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$. To enforce numerical stability and guarantee energy conservation across volatile connections, we utilize the Symmetric Normalized Graph Laplacian $L_{\text{sym}}$:

\begin{equation}
L_{\text{sym}} = D^{-1/2} L D^{-1/2} = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}
\end{equation}

where $A$ is the adjacency matrix, $D$ is the degree matrix, and $L$ is the unnormalized Laplacian. Because the eigenvalues of $L_{\text{sym}}$ are bounded strictly within $[0, 2]$, discrete-time signal propagation can be formulated as a contractive mapping:

\begin{equation}
\Psi_{t+1} = \text{proj}_{\mathcal{S}} \left[ \sigma \left( \left( (1 - \gamma)I - \alpha L_{\text{sym}} \right) \Psi_t + \mathbf{V}_{\text{ext}} \right) \right]
\end{equation}

Here, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ represents the scale diffusion step size, $\gamma$ is a damping coefficient tracking environmental entropy/packet loss, $\mathbf{V}_{\text{ext}}$ is the external macro-potential bias, and $\text{proj}_{\mathcal{S}}$ is a metric projection forcing the system state back onto a stable unit hypersphere $\mathcal{S}$.

\section{Non-Commutative Lattice Wetterich Flow}
To isolate the macro-topological computation from micro-scale hardware faults (e.g., thermal throttling, bit-flips), we implement a non-commutative lattice formulation of the Wetterich Exact Renormalization Group (ERG) equation. The functional trace is evaluated as a spectral trace over the discrete eigenspace of the graph:

\begin{equation}
\partial_k \Gamma_k = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{V}|} \left[ \left( \Gamma_k^{(2)} + R_k(L_{\text{sym}}) \right)^{-1} \partial_k R_k(L_{\text{sym}}) \right]_{ii}
\end{equation}

where $k = 1/s$ is the infrared momentum cutoff, $\Gamma_k^{(2)}$ is the second functional derivative of the average effective action, and $R_k(L_{\text{sym}})$ is a spectral heat-kernel regulator acting as an analytical low-pass filter:

\begin{equation}
    R_k(L_{\text{sym}}) = \frac{k^2}{\exp(L_{\text{sym}}^2 / k^2) - 1}
\end{equation}

As the cutoff parameter moves toward the macro-scale ($k \to 0$), the high-frequency fluctuations belonging to defective hardware vanish continuously from the global computation. Stable macro-memories are defined as the invariant fixed points of this flow where the beta function vanishes: $\beta(\Psi) = \partial \Psi / \partial \ln s = 0$.
